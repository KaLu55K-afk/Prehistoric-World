A PLANET IN MOTION

Timeline of deep time

Dates are approximate. Geological boundaries are refined as new evidence is found.

4.54 billion years ago

Earth forms

Earth accretes from material orbiting the young Sun. The planet heats, differentiates into layers, and gradually develops a crust and oceans.

At least 3.5 billion years ago

Early life

Microbial life is present. The earliest organisms were simple and did not resemble plants or animals. Their history is reconstructed from fossils, chemistry, and ancient rocks.

About 541 million years ago

Cambrian diversification

Many animal groups become conspicuous in the fossil record. This was not the instant origin of all life; evolution had already been happening for billions of years.

About 230 million years ago

First dinosaurs

Dinosaurs appear in the Triassic. They were initially one part of a diverse ecosystem and later became dominant land animals in many environments.

About 150 million years ago

Birds take flight

Early birds evolve from theropod dinosaurs. Birds are living dinosaurs, so the dinosaur lineage did not disappear completely.

66 million years ago

K–Pg mass extinction

A large asteroid impact contributed to a global environmental crisis. All non-avian dinosaurs went extinct, while birds and many other groups survived.

About 300,000 years ago

Homo sapiens

Our species evolves in Africa. Human evolution is branching, not a straight ladder, and our species later spreads across the world.

◉A BRANCHING FAMILY TREE

OUR OWN DEEP PAST

Human evolution is a story of branches.

Humans did not evolve from the monkeys alive today. Humans and other living primates share common ancestors. Over millions of years, populations changed and split into different lineages.

Important chapters

  • Early hominins: fossils show several species with different combinations of upright walking, climbing, and other traits.
  • Australopithecus: African hominins that walked upright but retained many climbing adaptations.
  • Homo erectus: a widespread human relative with body proportions suited to long-distance walking; some populations expanded beyond Africa.
  • Neanderthals and Denisovans: close relatives of modern humans. DNA shows that some ancestors of people today interbred with these groups.
  • Homo sapiens: our species, with the oldest widely accepted fossils dating to roughly 300,000 years ago in Africa.

Evolution has no predetermined goal. Traits become more or less common through processes such as natural selection, genetic drift, mutation, and migration.

FROM ROCK TO DISCOVERY

How fossils become evidence

Fossils preserve traces of past life. Bones, teeth, shells, pollen, footprints, burrows, and even chemical signals can reveal ancient ecosystems. Most organisms never fossilize, so the record is incomplete.

01

Burial

Rapid burial by mud, sand, ash, or other sediment can protect remains from scavengers and weathering.

02

Preservation

Minerals may fill pores or replace original material. Pressure and chemistry gradually turn sediment into rock.

03

Exposure

Uplift and erosion can expose fossil-bearing rock at the surface. Field teams map the location and geological layer.

04

Study and dating

Scientists document, carefully excavate, compare anatomy, and use stratigraphy and suitable radiometric methods to estimate ages.

HOW SCIENCE KNOWS

Evidence works together

A fossil's age is not usually read directly from the fossil itself. Researchers combine rock layers, volcanic ash dates when available, anatomical comparisons, geochemistry, and other evidence.

What can fossils tell us?

  • Body shape, growth, and injuries
  • Diet clues from teeth and stomach contents
  • Movement from trackways
  • Ancient climates and environments
  • Evolutionary relationships—often with uncertainty

Scientific explanations can change when new fossils or better analyses become available.
